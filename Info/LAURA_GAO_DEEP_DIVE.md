## 1. Executive brief

Laura Gao is unusually well suited to a goals-based portfolio narrative. Their career repeatedly combines two modes:

1. **Analytical builder:** Wharton statistics/business-analytics training; product management; A/B testing; recommendation systems; data visualization; web development; product design; and entrepreneurship.
2. **Community-centered storyteller:** comics about immigration, queer identity, Wuhan, family, and belonging; teaching and mentorship; nonprofit technology work; and organization of a free LGBTQ+ comics festival.

The planned Taiwan residency sits directly at the intersection of those modes. It is not a random philanthropic project. Gao has lived in Taipei, traveled around Taiwan, called Taiwan one of their favorite places to live and travel, taught creators, built community events, and publicly described a desire to create joyful queer media. A collaborative space for artists, writers, designers, entrepreneurs, and educators therefore extends an existing pattern in her life.

The investment problem is best framed as **protect the promise; grow the possibility**:

- The non-negotiable promise is ten fixed $50,000 operating payments from 2033 through 2042.
- The growth opportunity is the 2033 facility contribution.
- The credibility constraint arrives early: Gao must give co-sponsors a defensible contribution range in 2031.
- Financial flexibility matters because Gao's creative income is potentially lumpy and the facility itself is entrepreneurial.

A strong strategy should therefore avoid treating the whole portfolio as one undifferentiated growth pool. It should use a **two-horizon architecture**: a diversified growth portfolio before 2033, followed by a dedicated operating-reserve liability ladder and a separate facility/flexibility sleeve.

- Teams may not rely on grants, co-sponsors, program fees, or future business income for those payments.
- The ten payments are fixed nominal dollars and are not inflation-adjusted.

- The total facility cost is deliberately unspecified; teams recommend only what Gao can responsibly contribute.
- Taxes and Taiwan legal/regulatory details are outside competition scope.
- WInS trading gains or losses do not change the long-term projection's starting values.

### What the judges are really testing

The case is a small institutional asset-liability-management problem disguised as a personal portfolio. The strongest work will connect:

- a return-seeking allocation in 2027–2032;
- sequence-of-returns protection before the 2031 promise and 2033 withdrawal;
- a defensible definition of “high certainty”;
- a 2033 reserve that matches annual liabilities;
- an honest facility-contribution range rather than a single forecast;
- and language that potential co-sponsors can trust.

## 7. Quantitative case analysis

### Portfolio value at the beginning of 2033

Assuming a constant annual return and no fees, the $300,000 invested at the beginning of 2027 compounds for six years; the $150,000 added at the beginning of 2028 compounds for five years.

\[
V_{2033}=300{,}000(1+r)^6+150{,}000(1+r)^5
\]

| Constant nominal return | Beginning-2033 value |
|---:|---:|
| 0% | $450,000 |
| 3% | $532,107 |
| 4% | $562,094 |
| 5% | $593,471 |
| 6% | $626,290 |
| 7% | $660,602 |
| 8% | $696,462 |
| 9% | $733,924 |
| 10% | $773,045 |

These are illustrations, not forecasts. Real paths are volatile; a 7% average with a severe loss in 2031–2032 can produce a very different facility capacity than a smooth 7% path.

### Operating-reserve value in 2033

The first $50,000 payment is due immediately at the beginning of 2033, followed by nine annual payments. The reserve is therefore an **annuity due**:

\[
R_{2033}=50{,}000\sum_{k=0}^{9}(1+y)^{-k}
\]

| Reserve yield assumption | Required reserve at beginning 2033 |
|---:|---:|
| 0% | $500,000 |
| 2% | $458,112 |
| 3% | $439,305 |
| 4% | $421,767 |
| 5% | $405,391 |
| 6% | $390,085 |

Do not select the reserve merely by applying a hoped-for return. “High certainty” calls for assets whose cash flows match the dates—such as cash plus high-quality government bonds maturing before each payment—not a volatile balanced fund.

### Recommended definition of high certainty

Use two complementary tests:

1. **Cash-flow matching test:** At 2033, each $50,000 payment is matched by cash or a high-quality bond maturing before the payment date. This can support near-contractual certainty if default and reinvestment risks are tightly controlled.
2. **Simulation test:** Before 2033, at least 95%—preferably 97.5%—of modeled scenarios leave enough to purchase the full reserve. Stress cases should include an equity crash, high inflation, rate shocks, and poor returns in 2030–2032.

This definition is more credible than claiming “100% guaranteed.” Even government bonds face operational, currency, and reinvestment considerations.

## 8. Strategy implications and a defensible architecture

### Phase 1: 2027–2030 — diversified growth with guardrails

The portfolio has no withdrawals before 2033 and Gao has outside resources for living costs. A growth-oriented diversified allocation is reasonable, but the seven-year horizon to the first liability is not long enough for an all-equity gamble.

An illustrative starting policy could use roughly **60–70% global equities and 30–40% high-quality fixed income/cash**, subject to the competition's eligible-security list. Diversify across U.S. and international markets, styles, and sectors. Avoid thematic concentration in technology, China/Taiwan, publishing, or entertainment.

### Phase 2: 2031–2032 — protect the promise

The 2031 co-sponsor conversation creates an earlier risk date than the 2033 withdrawal. Begin de-risking before that conversation:

- establish a “reserve funding ratio” equal to portfolio value earmarked for operations divided by the cost of the intended liability-matching reserve;
- transfer gains above a predetermined threshold into short/intermediate high-quality bonds;
- reduce equity exposure as the reserve becomes fully fundable;
- base the co-sponsor range on the 2031 distribution of 2033 outcomes, not on the portfolio's single current value.

### Phase 3: 2033–2042 — separate the buckets

1. **Operating reserve:** cash for the immediate payment plus a rolling ladder of high-quality government bonds or similarly reliable instruments matched to 2034–2042 payments.
2. **Facility contribution:** paid only after the reserve is fully established.
3. **Flexible remainder:** retained for contingencies and future opportunity; invested according to its longer horizon.

### Facility-contribution rule

Use a formula rather than a fixed aspiration:

\[
\text{Facility contribution}=\max\{0,V_{2033}-R_{operations}-F_{flexibility}\}
\]

where `F_flexibility` is a policy buffer, not an attempt to estimate facility cost. A buffer of roughly 10–20% of the portfolio after funding the operating reserve is a reasonable discussion range, but must be justified through scenario analysis.

At a smooth 6% path, the portfolio reaches about $626,290. Against a 3–4% reserve value of about $422,000–$439,000, only $187,000–$205,000 remains before a flexibility buffer. This arithmetic is crucial: the facility contribution cannot responsibly be assumed to be enormous.

### Co-sponsor range

The final range should come from Monte Carlo percentiles and should be updated in 2031. A defensible method is:

- lower bound: facility capacity in the 10th or 20th percentile after fully funding operations and flexibility;
- upper bound: facility capacity around the 70th or 80th percentile, not the most optimistic tail;
- confidence: probability that the ultimate contribution lies inside the stated range;
- downside clause: operating commitment remains fully protected even below the lower facility range.

Do not promise a precise range until the team has run simulations using explicit capital-market assumptions. A preliminary planning range might be approximately **$75,000–$200,000**, but this is a hypothesis to test, not a completed recommendation.

### Sample fundraising language

> Laura expects to contribute approximately **$[lower] to $[upper]** toward establishing the Taiwan creative residency in 2033. This range is based on diversified portfolio projections and is designed to preserve a separately funded reserve for all ten annual operating commitments. We estimate a **[X]% probability** that the contribution will fall within this range. Stronger markets may permit a contribution near the upper end; under weaker conditions, Laura will protect the operating reserve and financial flexibility before increasing the facility commitment.

This language signals meaningful personal commitment without presenting market-dependent money as guaranteed.

- **Tech overexposure:** Gao's education, former employment, creative tools, audience, and income already depend heavily on technology.

- **One average-return forecast:** Sequence risk makes a single compounded number inadequate.
- **Overpromising in 2031:** Credibility with co-sponsors is itself an asset.

## 11. Less-obvious insights

1. **The residency is a convergence of past selves.** It combines the Wharton organizer, Twitter product manager, freelance entrepreneur, global traveler, teacher, author, and queer-community builder.
2. **2031—not 2033—is the practical risk horizon.** A market decline after Gao makes a public commitment can damage credibility even if assets later recover.
3. **The operating reserve is easier than it looks only because payments are nominal.** Inflation erodes the real value of $50,000, which helps portfolio solvency but may squeeze the residency's real operating power. Teams must discuss this distinction.
4. **Facility inflation remains a hidden threat.** The case says not to estimate total facility cost, but it explicitly asks teams to state assumptions about inflation and facility costs. A fixed-dollar contribution may buy less in 2033.
5. **Gao's risk capacity and risk need differ.** Outside resources and no pre-2033 withdrawals create capacity for volatility; the fixed operating promise reduces the need to take maximum risk.
6. **Flexibility has option value.** Holding back capital in 2033 is not lack of commitment. It protects Gao's ability to respond to overruns, partnership changes, new creative opportunities, or facility redesign.
7. **Trust is part of return.** A slightly lower but credible co-sponsor range may attract more durable support than an impressive but fragile promise.
8. **A creator-aligned portfolio should diversify away from creator economics.** Personalization comes from goal design and stewardship rules, not from doubling down on the client's career sector.
